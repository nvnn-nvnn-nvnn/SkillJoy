# 00 — Vision & Positioning

## The shift

| | v1 (today) | v2 (rebrand) |
|---|---|---|
| **Tagline** | Campus skill-swap & gig marketplace | Sell your digital products & services — for everyday creators |
| **Who** | Verified college students (`.edu`) | Anyone who creates |
| **What they sell** | Their time (custom gigs) or skills (barter) | **Digital products** (sold many times) + **custom services** |
| **Fulfillment** | Negotiated, escrowed, delivered per buyer | Products: instant download/license. Services: per-buyer (escrow). |
| **Money** | Escrow-heavy, dispute-heavy | Mostly direct charge; escrow only for custom services |

## Who v2 is for

"**Everyday creators**" — not agencies, not only students. People with something
digital to sell: a Notion template, a Lightroom preset pack, an ebook, a sample
pack, a course, a design file, a membership. Plus those who still want to offer
custom work (a logo, an edit, a tutoring session).

## The Gumroad comparison (and where we differ)

**Like Gumroad:**
- A creator lists a product once; it sells many times.
- Delivery is automatic — buyer pays, instantly gets the file/license.
- No bidding, no per-buyer negotiation for products.
- Simple flat/percentage fee per sale.

**Unlike Gumroad — we keep a services lane:**
- v1 already has a robust per-buyer **escrow + dispute** system. Rather than
  throw it away, v2 reframes it as the home for *custom services*. This is a
  real differentiator: one profile can sell a $9 template **and** a $200 custom
  commission.

## What success looks like for the rebrand

1. A new user lands on the site and immediately understands "I can sell digital
   stuff here," with no campus framing.
2. A creator can publish a downloadable product in minutes.
3. A buyer can purchase and download it without a request/accept handshake.
4. The old swap/dispute machinery is invisible to new users but intact for
   future use.

## Non-goals (for now)

- No name change (stays SkillJoy).
- No deletion of swaps, disputes, or college verification.
- No new payment processor — Stripe stays.
- No mobile app — web only, as today.
